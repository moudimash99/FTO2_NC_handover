What the tab is for

The Pareto tab answers the question what goes wrong, and how much? It sorts the defects of the selection from the largest group to the smallest, from several angles, and lets you follow one cause down to the defects themselves. It counts defects, not NCs: an NC with four defects counts four times. It shows the NCs raised at FTO2 that FTO2 is liable for, under the filters at the top of the page.

Two switches sit above the charts. Measure: Volume (defects) counts the defects, Cost of non-quality their cost; the tab opens on the cost, and a cause can be small in number but large in cost. Cable defects in the bars: Shown marks, inside each bar, the part that comes from a cable defect.

The Pareto cards

Category

ShowsThe nine families of defects, from the largest to the smallest, with the defects, the NCs and the running share (Cumul).
How to read it"65 %" in Cumul on the second row: the first two categories make 65 % of the defects. In the NC column, an NC is shared among its defects, hence the decimals.
How it helpsThe big picture in one look: where most of the effort pays off.
A clickFilters the other charts of this tab, and its list.

Root cause (RCA)

ShowsThe same defects by cause: 25 causes, each bar coloured by its category.
How to read itOne row per cause, the largest first.
How it helpsThe card to start from when you want to act: a cause says what went wrong (a swapped wire, a missing plug, a damaged cable).
A clickFilters the other charts of this tab, and its list.

How to read itOne row per line.
How it helpsClick a cause first: this card then tells where it happens.

Official sub-code

ShowsThe defects by sub-code of the official code sheet (Q11-003, T04…).
How to read itOne row per sub-code.
How it helpsThe most precise level, and the one SAP and the code sheet use: useful with other teams or to compare with an official report.
A clickFilters the other charts of this tab, and its list.

FIN (reference)

ShowsThe 20 references named most often in the defects.
How to read itDefects with no FIN written are left out, and so are product names written where a FIN should be (740VU…).
How it helpsA FIN that stays high points to one connector or equipment where the same problem repeats: often the quickest fix.
A clickFilters the other charts of this tab, and its list.

The other charts

A hundred defects

ShowsA hundred squares, each 1 % of the defects, coloured by category.
How to read itCount the squares of a colour: that is its share in %.
How it helpsThe shares at a glance; easy to show in a meeting.

Volume or unit cost

ShowsOne bubble per cause.
How to read itFurther right: more frequent. Higher: more expensive per defect. Bigger: more costly in total. The dotted curves join causes of equal total cost.
How it helpsHelps choose the kind of action: to the right and low, a volume problem; high and to the left, a unit-cost problem.
A clickKeeps that cause.

Monthly ranking of root causes

ShowsThe rank of the eight main causes, month after month.
How to read itA line climbing towards rank 1 is a cause taking more room; a grey band marks a month with few defects.
How it helpsShows trends that a total for the year hides.

Root causes month by month

ShowsA few causes stacked in one bar per month, month after month: the top three of the selection, plus causes added by hand. Unlocked contact and FOD are there from the start, for safety.
How to read it− and + change how many top causes are shown; ✕ removes a cause added by hand. The measure is the tab's (defects or cost); a switch on the card counts NCs instead. Two causes of one category share its colour, one lighter. The month in progress is pale.

A cause per MSN, month by month

ShowsOne cause per aircraft built, month by month: the cause you follow in Monthly ranking of root causes (click its line); without a click, the first cause of the last month.
How to read itLine: the average of the period; dotted: three-month average; red dashed: that cause's rate last year. The fine dotted lines are the 2σ and 3σ limits: the average plus two or three times the usual spread from one month to the next; a month above 3σ is hardly ever chance. A330 and A350 when all lines are shown, else the line of the filter.
How it helpsShows whether the cause you are working on improves, whatever the change in volume.
A clickKeeps that month.

How the cards work together

A click on a bar keeps only that part in the other cards and in the list: a cause, then a line, then a FIN narrows down step by step. A label above the cards shows each choice; Clear all removes them. These clicks only filter this tab: the filters at the top of the page stay as they are.

Good to know

  • The A320 is left out of the "per aircraft built" charts: its products are small parts counted one by one and would pull the rates down.
  • A cause name you are unsure of: the Definitions tab gives its definition and real examples.
  • The ⤓ Image button, at the top right of each chart, saves it as an image, with its title and key, ready for a presentation.
