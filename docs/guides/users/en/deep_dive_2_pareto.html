What the tab is for

The Pareto tab answers the question what goes wrong, and how much? It sorts the defects of the selection from the largest group to the smallest, from several angles, and lets you follow one cause down to the defects themselves. It counts defects, not NCs: an NC with four defects counts four times. It shows the NCs raised at FTO2 that FTO2 is liable for, under the filters at the top of the page.

The two switches

Measure chooses what the bars count: Volume (defects), the number of defects, or Cost of non-quality, their cost. The tab opens on the cost. Looking at both is useful: a cause can be small in number but large in cost, or the other way round. Cable defects in the bars marks, inside each bar, the part that comes from a cable defect, which shows at once how much of a category is a cable problem.

The charts

Category

The nine families of defects, from the largest to the smallest, with the number of defects, the number of NCs and the running share (Cumul). It gives the big picture in one look: when the first two categories make 65 % of the defects, that is where most of the effort pays off. In the NC column, an NC is shared among its defects, which is why it shows decimals.

Root cause (RCA)

The same defects by cause, one level finer than the category: 25 causes, each bar coloured by its category. This is the card to start from when you want to act, because a cause points to what went wrong (a swapped wire, a missing plug, a damaged cable) rather than to a broad family.

Line

The same defects by aircraft line. It shows which line carries most of the defects of a cause: click a cause first, and this card tells you where it happens.

Official sub-code

The defects by sub-code of the official code sheet (Q11-003, T04…). It is the most precise level and the one SAP and the code sheet use, which helps when you talk with other teams or compare with an official report.

FIN (reference)

The 20 references named most often in the defects. A FIN that stays high points to a specific connector or equipment where the same problem repeats, which is often the quickest thing to fix. Defects with no FIN written are left out.

A hundred defects

A hundred squares, each 1 % of the defects, coloured by category. It gives the shares at a glance, and is easy to show in a meeting.

Volume or unit cost

One bubble per cause: the further right, the more frequent; the higher, the more expensive each defect is; the bigger, the more it costs in total. It helps to choose the right kind of action: a cause to the right and low is a matter of volume (many small defects), a cause high and to the left a matter of unit cost (few defects, each expensive).

Monthly ranking of root causes

The rank of the eight main causes, month after month. A line climbing towards rank 1 is a cause taking more room; a line going down is a cause being brought under control. It shows trends that a single total hides. Click a line to follow it.

From where it is found to the decision

The flow of the defects from where the NC was found (final inspection, test, cables, internal), through their category, to the decision taken on the NC. The thicker the band, the more defects. It shows, for example, which categories are only caught at final inspection, late and costly, and which ones end in a rejection rather than a rework.

Connection errors

Three charts on swapped wires, wrong terminals and wires not connected: per aircraft built, month by month; per product and cause; and per million connection operations on the MIP A350, against its target. Connection errors are a large and well-defined family, and these charts show whether they improve, on the A330 and the A350.

Root causes: 2025 against 2026

For the main causes of each year, the defects per aircraft built last year and this year, joined by a line. A line going up is a cause that grew since last year, whatever the change in production volume. A330 and A350.

Root causes against volume

The causes ranked in defects per aircraft built. Dividing by the volume makes causes comparable across lines that build very different numbers of aircraft.

Other charts

Under ▸ Other charts: cable defects per aircraft built, month by month; and Which pins get swapped, which draws, for the connectors where swaps are most frequent, the pairs of pins that get swapped. The second one often shows that the same two neighbouring pins are swapped aircraft after aircraft.

Defect and cost detail

At the bottom of the tab, the list behind every chart: one row per defect, with its NC, date, line, category, cause, sub-code, its share of the NC's cost, and its text as written in the NC. It is where a figure becomes real cases. Search it by NC number, word or FIN.

How the charts work together

A click on a bar keeps only that part in the other charts and in the list. Clicking a cause, then a line, then a FIN narrows down step by step to the defects that matter. A label above the charts shows each choice; Clear all removes them. These clicks only filter this tab: the filters at the top of the page stay as they are.

Good to know

  • The A320 is left out of the "per aircraft built" charts: its products are small parts counted one by one and would pull the rates down.
  • A cause name you are unsure of: the Definitions tab gives its definition and real examples.
