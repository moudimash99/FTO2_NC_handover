What the tab is for

The Pareto tab answers: what goes wrong, and how much? It sorts the defects of the selection from the largest group to the smallest, from five angles, and lets you follow one cause down to the defects themselves. It counts defects, not NCs: an NC with four defects counts four times.

The two switches at the top

  • Measure: Volume (defects) counts the defects; Cost of non-quality counts their cost. The tab opens on the cost. Every card follows the switch.
  • Cable defects in the bars: Shown marks, inside each bar, the part that is a cable defect.

The five Pareto cards

CardWhat it shows
CategoryThe nine families of defects, from the largest to the smallest. The columns give the defects, the NCs and Cumul, the running share: "65 %" on the second row means the first two categories make 65 % of the defects.
Root cause (RCA)The same defects by cause (25 causes). In each bar, the colours are the categories.
LineThe same defects by aircraft line.
Official sub-codeBy sub-code of the official code sheet (Q11-003, T04…).
FIN (reference)The 20 references named most often in the defects. Defects with no FIN written are left out.

In the NC column, an NC is shared among its defects: one defect of an NC that has four counts for a quarter of an NC. This is why that column shows decimals.

Following one cause

  1. Click a bar, for example the cause Swapped wires in Root cause (RCA). It keeps its colour, the others turn grey, and a label appears above the cards.
  2. The other cards now count that cause only: on which lines, which sub-codes, which FINs.
  3. Click in a second card to narrow further, for example a line, then a FIN.
  4. Open ▸ Defect and cost detail at the bottom: one row per defect, with its NC, date, line, category, cause, sub-code, its share of the NC's cost, and its text. Search it by NC number, word or FIN.
  5. Clear all, beside the labels, removes these choices. They only filter this tab: the filters at the top stay as they are.

The other charts of the tab

ChartWhat it answers
A hundred defectsA hundred squares, each 1 % of the defects, coloured by category: the shares at a glance.
Volume or unit costOne bubble per cause. To the right, frequent; towards the top, expensive per defect; the size is the total cost. A cause to the right and low is a volume problem; high and to the left, a unit-cost problem.
Monthly ranking of root causesThe rank of the eight main causes, month after month. A line climbing towards rank 1 is a cause taking more room. Click a line to follow it.
From where it is found to the decisionThe flow of the defects: where the NC was found (final inspection, test, cables, internal), the category, then the decision taken. The thicker the band, the more defects.
Connection errors (three charts)Swapped wires, wrong terminals and wires not connected: per MSN month by month, per product, and per million connection operations on the MIP A350. A330 and A350 only.
Root causes: 2025 against 2026Defects per MSN for the main causes of each year: a line going up is a cause that grew. A330 and A350.
Root causes against volumeThe causes ranked in defects per MSN, so lines of different volume compare fairly.
▸ Other chartsCable defects per MSN month by month, and which pins get swapped on the connectors where it happens most.

Good to know

  • The Pareto counts NCs raised at FTO2 that FTO2 is liable for, deleted ones included, under the filters at the top.
  • The A320 is left out of the "per MSN" charts: its products are small parts counted one by one and would pull the rates down.
  • A cause name you are unsure of: the Definitions tab gives its definition and real examples.
