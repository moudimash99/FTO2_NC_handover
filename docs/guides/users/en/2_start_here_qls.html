What you look for

You follow one line. You want to know what goes wrong on it, whether it gets better or worse, and where to act: which cause, which product, which FIN, which station, which aircraft. The dashboard answers this month by month. The weekly review is done on the MFT page, which has its own guide.

If you have not used the dashboard yet, read the Introduction to the dashboard first (ten minutes).

First, choose your line

In the filter Aircraft, choose your line. Every tab now counts your line only, and so does the bell. Leave the other filters as the page opens (series NCs, raised at FTO2, FTO2 liable, since 1 January), unless you look at REC or FTI on purpose.

Your monthly routine

  1. Essential › Detail. The card Category shows what weighs most on your line. Monthly ranking of root causes shows which causes climb or fall from month to month. The top three root causes of each product tells you what to work on, product by product.
  2. The bell. Open it: the alarms now concern your line only. Click one to see the NCs behind it.
  3. Deep dive › Pareto. Click the cause that worries you in the card Root cause (RCA): the other cards show its FINs, its official sub-codes and its share of each product. Open ▸ Defect and cost detail at the bottom to read the defects one by one.
  4. Deep dive › Location. FINs that come back aircraft after aircraft shows the references that repeat; Cost per aircraft (MSN) shows the aircraft far above the others; Defects by station and category shows where the NCs are written.
  5. Deep dive › Customer returns, with your line chosen: what the assembly lines and the customers sent back.

Good to know

  • Read rates, not only counts. A month with more aircraft built has more NCs. NCs per MSN divides by the volume built, so months can be compared. July and August count for about 60 % of a month, because of the shutdown.
  • The month in progress is judged on the days gone by: its dot is hollow, and it will still move.
  • A cause looks wrong for a defect? Check its definition in the Definitions tab. If it really is wrong, give the NC number to the person in charge of the dashboard. An official sub-code written in the NC always decides the cause.
