What you look for

You follow one line. You want to know what goes wrong on it, whether it gets better or worse, and where to act: which cause, which product, which FIN, which station, which aircraft. The dashboard answers this month by month. The weekly review is done on the MFT page, which has its own guide.

If you have not used the dashboard yet, read the Introduction to the dashboard first (ten minutes).

Looking at your line only

The filter Aircraft keeps one line: with your line chosen, every tab counts your line only, and so does the bell. The other filters can stay as the page opens (series NCs, raised at FTO2, FTO2 liable, since 1 January).

The charts that help you most

Essential › Detail was made for the review of a line. Category shows what weighs most on your line. Monthly ranking of root causes shows which causes climb or fall from one month to the next, which a total for the year hides. The top three root causes of each product tells you, product by product, where an action would remove the most defects. A cause per MSN, month by month follows over time the cause you click in the monthly ranking. Root causes month by month follows a few causes side by side, with Unlocked contact and FOD always in view.

Deep dive › Pareto lets you follow one cause down to its FINs, its official sub-codes and the defects themselves, with their text. It is where a figure becomes real cases.

Deep dive › Location shows where the problems sit: FINs that come back aircraft after aircraft points to the references where the same defect repeats; Cost per aircraft (MSN) shows the aircraft far above the others; Defects by station and category shows at which stations the defects are written.

The bell watches your line week by week and points to what is rising more than chance can explain. Deep dive › Customer returns shows what the assembly lines and the customers sent back for your line.

Good to know

  • Read rates, not only counts. A month with more aircraft built has more NCs. NCs per MSN divides by the volume built, so months can be compared. A month with closures (summer, Christmas) counts for the days the plant works.
  • The month in progress is judged on the days gone by: its dot is hollow, and it will still move.
  • A cause looks wrong for a defect? Check its definition in the Definitions tab. If it really is wrong, give the NC number to the person in charge of the dashboard. A code written in the NC wins: with the NC writer, its official code gives the cause.
