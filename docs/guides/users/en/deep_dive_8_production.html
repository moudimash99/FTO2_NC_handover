What the tab is for

Most figures of the dashboard are rates: NCs divided by the aircraft and products built. The Production tab shows those volumes: how many were built, planned and delivered, line by line and product by product, and how they feed the rates, the forecasts and the targets. It is the place to look when a rate surprises.

The charts and tables

2025 and 2026, month by month

Three rows of bars, the two years side by side: the NCs, the aircraft built, and the NCs per aircraft. A switch shows A330 + A350 or A320. It shows whether a change in the rate comes from the NCs or from the volume.

By line

The table behind the targets: for each line, the aircraft built and planned this year, delivered last year, the NCs of both years, the rate, and the target, in NCs per aircraft and in number of NCs. It shows exactly how each line's target was built.

MSN planned, month by month

The production plan of the year by line; lighter colours are the months to come. It shows the volume ahead, which drives the December forecast.

By product

The same table for each product of the production plan. The few NCs whose product could not be matched to the plan are listed under it: they count in every total, but not in the rates.

How these figures are made and used

A card that explains, in words and with the figures of the day, where the volumes come from and how they are used.

How the volumes are made

  • This year: from the production plan. Each aircraft or product counts for 1, spread over the working days between the start and the end of its build. A month's volume is the work really done that month.
  • Working days are Monday to Friday, without public holidays and blackout days. This is why July and August count for about 60 % of a month. The month in progress counts for the days gone by.
  • Last year: the products delivered each month.
  • The target of a line is its rate of last year, lowered by the cut decided for the year, multiplied by its production of this year. The forecast is the rate so far multiplied by the aircraft still planned.
