What the tab is for

The Location tab answers: where do the NCs come from? It looks at the same NCs by product, by aircraft and by station, and sets them against the volume built wherever it can, so that a product built often is not unfairly at the top. It has three parts: Products, Aircraft, Stations.

Products

NCs, defects and cost expected by December, product by product

For each product, what is done so far, then what is expected at the end of December if the pace stays the same. The switch Per MSN shows the same per aircraft built. It helps to see which products will weigh most on the year, not only which weigh most today.

Monthly rate, product by product

A grid with one row per product and one column per month: the darker the cell, the more NCs per aircraft built that month. A dash means less than half an aircraft was built that month, so no rate. It shows at once whether a product is always high or had one bad month.

Cost by product family

The ten costliest product families, with their number of NCs, of aircraft, and the average cost per aircraft. It points to the families where non-quality costs most for each aircraft built.

The top three root causes of each product

For each product, from the most affected to the least, its three main causes in defects per aircraft built. It tells where an action would remove the most defects, product by product.

Aircraft

Cost per aircraft (MSN)

One bar per aircraft, in the order of production, line by line, with the average of the line as a dotted line. The aircraft far above the others are named. Hatched bars are aircraft still in production, whose cost can still grow; pale bars are aircraft started before this year. It shows whether the problems are spread, or concentrated on a few aircraft. A click on a bar shows the NCs of that aircraft.

FINs that come back aircraft after aircraft

One column per aircraft, one row per FIN; a square is a defect on that FIN on that aircraft, a dark square two or more. A row full of squares is a defect that repeats on almost every aircraft: a strong candidate for an action. It shows the line chosen in the filter, or the A350 when all lines are shown.

Stations

Defects by station and category

The twelve stations whose NCs carry the most defects, split by category. The station is the one where the NC was written. It helps to see whether a kind of defect is found mostly at one place.

Other charts and the list

Under ▸ Other charts, How far can each product's rate be trusted? shows each product's rate with its margin of uncertainty: wide when few aircraft were built, so a high rate on few aircraft may be chance. At the bottom, NC detail: product, aircraft, station lists every NC; in this list, a click on a line, product, MSN or station filters the whole page.
