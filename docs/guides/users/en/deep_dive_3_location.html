What the tab is for

The Location tab answers: where do the NCs come from? It looks at the same NCs by product, by aircraft and by station, and sets them against the volume built wherever it can, so that a product built often is not unfairly at the top. It has three parts: Products, Aircraft, Stations.

Products

NCs, defects and cost expected by December, product by product

ShowsFor each product, what is done so far, then what is expected at the end of December.
How to read itSolid bar: done. Dotted part: the forecast (the rate of the period × the aircraft still planned). The switch Per MSN shows the same per aircraft built.
How it helpsShows which products will weigh most on the year, not only which weigh most today.
A clickKeeps that product on the whole page.

Monthly rate, product by product

ShowsA grid: one row per product, one column per month.
How to read itThe darker the cell, the more NCs per aircraft built that month. A dash: less than half an aircraft built, so no rate.
How it helpsShows whether a product is always high, or had one bad month.
A clickKeeps that product on the whole page.

Cost by product family

ShowsThe ten costliest product families, with their NCs, aircraft and average cost per aircraft.
How to read itThe rest is grouped in one row.
How it helpsPoints to the families where non-quality costs most for each aircraft built.
A clickKeeps that product family on the whole page.

The top three root causes of each product

ShowsFor each product, its three main causes, in defects per aircraft built.
How to read itProducts from the most affected to the least.
How it helpsTells, product by product, where an action would remove the most defects.
A clickKeeps that cause.

Aircraft

Cost per aircraft (MSN)

ShowsOne bar per aircraft, in the order of production, line by line.
How to read itDotted line: the average of the line. Hatched bars: aircraft still in production, whose cost can still grow. Pale bars: aircraft started before this year. The aircraft far above the others are named.
How it helpsShows whether the problems are spread, or concentrated on a few aircraft.
A clickKeeps the NCs of that aircraft.

FINs that come back aircraft after aircraft

ShowsOne column per aircraft, one row per FIN.
How to read itA square: a defect on that FIN on that aircraft; a dark square: two or more. The line chosen in the filter, or the A350 when all lines are shown.
How it helpsA row full of squares is a defect that repeats on almost every aircraft: a strong candidate for an action.
A clickKeeps that FIN.

Stations

Defects by station and category

ShowsThe twelve stations whose NCs carry the most defects, split by category.
How to read itThe station is the one where the NC was written.
How it helpsShows whether a kind of defect is found mostly at one place.
A clickKeeps that station.

How far can each product's rate be trusted?Each product's rate with its margin of uncertainty: wide when few aircraft were built, so a high rate on few aircraft may be chance.

NC detail: product, aircraft, station

ShowsThe list behind every chart of the tab, the most recent first: date, line, product, MSN, station, type, cost and title.
How to read itSearch it by NC number, word or FIN. It shows 200 rows at a time; Show 200 more at its end shows more.
How it helpsIt is where a figure becomes real cases. In this list, a click on a line, a product, an MSN or a station filters the whole page.
