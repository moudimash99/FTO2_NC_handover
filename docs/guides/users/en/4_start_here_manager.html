What you look for

You want to know, in a few minutes, whether the year is on target, which line needs attention, and what non-quality costs. Everything you need is on the first tab the page opens: Essential › Summary.

If you have not used the dashboard yet, read the Introduction to the dashboard first (ten minutes).

The four key figures

FigureWhat it says
NCs since JanuaryThe NCs of the year. Under it: the number expected at the end of December if the pace stays the same, and the target of the year.
NCs per MSNNCs divided by the aircraft and products built. Under it: how far this is from the target, in red when above.
Cost of non-qualityThe estimated cost of these NCs, with its December forecast and its target.
Customer returnsNCs raised outside FTO2 for which FTO2 is liable, and their cost. Counted apart from the rest.

Two charts that sum up the year

  • Cumulative NCs over the year: the curve of the NCs month after month, against the dashed line of the target. Above the dashed line, the year is above its target at this date. The dotted line is the forecast to December.
  • Each line against its target: one bar per line; a bar past its black tick is a line above its target.

For the cost, the same curve exists in euros: Deep dive › Overview › Cumulative cost of non-quality. What the cost is made of, month by month, is in Deep dive › Cost of non-quality.

How the target and the cost are made

  • The target of a line is its rate of last year (NCs per product delivered), lowered by the cut decided for the year, 25 % today, multiplied by its production of this year. The target of all lines is the sum of the lines.
  • The forecast is the rate of the year so far multiplied by the aircraft still planned to December.
  • The cost is an estimate: the time each NC takes (alert, writing, support, preparation, rework, recheck), at the hourly rate of who does the work, plus engineering studies and parts. It is made to compare causes, lines and months; it is not an accounting figure.

What only you provide

A few values are decided, not computed. They come from you and are changed by the person in charge of the dashboard:

  • the year followed (changed each January) and the cut of the target;
  • the hourly rates, and the minutes each step of an NC takes;
  • the blackout days: the days the plant is closed or half closed;
  • the products delivered last year, and the planned volume of each product;
  • the plant followed.

When one of them changes, the figures of the whole year follow at the next update.
