Two kinds of settings

Almost everything that can change in the dashboard is a setting, not code. There are two kinds:

  • The values people fill in by hand are in the Fusion spreadsheet FTO2 NC Dashboard - Settings. Each sheet is synced to a dataset of its own (fto2_set_…), which the update reads.
  • The rules and the technical tables (which NC counts, which product, which cause, the cost model's details) are small tables kept with the code, in the settings folder. They change rarely, and only with care.

A change shows on the dashboard after the next update. Nothing is changed in the pages themselves.

Part 1 – The Head of Quality's sheets

The decided values. They are few, and each one moves figures of the whole year.

SheetWhat it holdsWhen it changes
Target and pagesThe cut of the target (0.25 = 25 % fewer NCs per product than the comparison year), what the pages show when they open, the target of an MFT action.When the target or the review changes.
Cost rates and timesThe hourly rates of production, quality and methods, and the minutes each step of an NC takes.When a rate or a process time changes.
Blackout daysThe days the plant is closed (weight 0) or half closed (weight 0.5), one row per period. The volume of a month is spread over its working days, so these days decide the volume of each month and week, the share of the month in progress, and the closure weeks every page leaves out of its averages. A day with no row is a full working day: the update warns when the year, or its Christmas, has no closure.Each year, Christmas included, and when a closure is added.

Three values are not settings any more. The year followed is the current year, and it changes by itself on 1 January. The volumes come from the production plan: last year's too, measured the same way (products built over the working days), except 2025, which the plan does not reach (0_livraisons_2025.csv, its deliveries). The plant is written in several rules and in the pages: changing it is a developer's task.

Because the year changes on 1 January, the dashboard starts almost empty in early January; that is normal. The bell stays quiet until the year has 16 finished weeks (late April).

The sheets filled in by hand: keep the first row (the column names) exactly as it is; one row per item, no merged cells, no totals; a date written as 2026-05-15 or 15/05/2026.

The last four sheets are pasted whole, as they are: empty the sheet, copy everything at the source (Ctrl+A, Ctrl+C in Google Sheets or in the export) and paste it in cell A1. Delete nothing and rename nothing: the update finds the header row itself, among the first rows, and leaves out the title rows above it and the columns it does not need. The plan's date is a date written above the export's header if there is one, else the day of the update.

How the update reads the sheets

  • A sheet is used when its dataset can be read by the update (in the notebook: it is in the Data panel). Otherwise the file of the settings folder with the same content is used, and the update says so at its start.
  • The column names are matched loosely (case, accents, spaces), because a sync may write them a little differently. A sheet with a column missing is not used: the update names the column and keeps the file.
  • A sheet with its header only is not used: the file is kept rather than emptied.
  • The first lines printed by the update list which settings came from Fusion, and for each sheet pasted whole, on which row it found the header and how many rows it took (Fusion sheet GPS register: header on row 4, 812 rows). A sheet it cannot use gets a warning, NOT USED, with the reason (the columns it did not find) and what it kept instead; the warning comes again at the end. Read them after any change.
  • The sheet Target and pages can also be read by the pages themselves (an option of the Skywise set-up: its README, step 3). A change there then shows the next time a page is opened, without waiting for the update. A line missing or wrongly written keeps the update's value.

The rule tables

The rules live in the settings folder, as small tables that open in Excel. Their name says who should touch them:

Starts withWhat it is
0_History and fallbacks: 2025's deliveries, the production plan as last read. Not edited by hand.
1_The values of the two sheets above, as files: the fallback when Fusion cannot be read. Also the MFT actions.
2_The rules and names: which NCs count, the line of each programme, the product of each NC, its type, its origin, the cause names, the categories and their colours, the names of the source datasets and files.
3_Expert: the cost model's details, the bell's settings, the official sub-codes.

The table lisez_moi.csv lists every table, what it changes and which part of the code reads it.

How a rule table works

Each NC is tested against the rules in the order of the column ordre. The rows of one rule must all be true; the first rule that holds gives the result; the rule marked toujours is the default. A test reads one column of the NC data: equal to, one of a list, contains, starts with, or a pattern.

So to send a new programme to its line, add one rule to 2_regle_ligne.csv; to leave out a kind of NC, add one to 2_regle_exclusion.csv. Put the new rule before a broader one, or the broader one wins first.

A mistake in a setting

The update reads every setting before it fetches anything. A badly filled value stops it within seconds, with nothing written, and with a plain message naming the table, the line and what to fix, for example 1_cout_parametres.csv, taux_qualite: 'abc' is not a number. Fix the line and run the update again.
