Two rules that explain most access problems

  • A reader needs the application and the tables it reads (the four tables, the settings and the MFT actions). The page reads the tables with the rights of the person who opens it: someone who can open the application but not the tables gets an empty page.
  • The update runs with the rights of the person who runs it. That person must be able to read every source and write the four tables. The source data belongs to other teams: access is requested from their owners, and it can take days. Ask early.

Giving access

Open the folder that holds the object, select it, choose Share, add the person or the group, and choose the role. To check a new reader: they open the dashboard link and see the date Données au… with figures. A box naming a query, or an empty page, means the four tables are missing from their rights.

Before someone leaves

A dashboard stops the day its only owner's account is closed. Go through this list with the successor at the keyboard, at least a week before the departure:

  1. Nothing is in a personal folder. What was is moved into the team's folder, and the dashboard is opened once after the move to check it still shows data.
  2. At least two owners on the folder, the leaving person not counted, one of them a manager.
  3. The successor has their own access to the three source datasets, and has run the update once with their own account.
  4. The successor opens the application in edit mode and sees the queries and the code of each page.
  5. The schedule of the update runs under an account that stays, or a project's service account.
  6. The leaving person's rights are removed afterwards, from the same places or from the group.
