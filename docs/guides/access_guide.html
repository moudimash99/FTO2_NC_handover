What needs access

The dashboard is not one object in Skywise but a chain of five. A person who lacks access to one link of the chain sees an empty page or an error. This guide lists the links, says who needs which, and gives the checks to make before the dashboard changes hands.

Who needs what

Four roles. Most people are readers; one person is in charge; one person can maintain the code. Give each role to a group of people rather than to one name whenever Skywise lets you.

RoleWhoWhat they do
ReaderThe team, the managers.Open the dashboard and the MFT page.
Person in chargeOne person of the team, and a deputy.Run the notebook, save the downloaded files, change the settings (the Settings guide).
MaintainerThe person who takes over the code.Change the program or the pages (the Technical manual).
OwnerAt least two people, one of them a manager.Give and remove access. Nothing else is asked of them.

Two rules that explain most access problems
  • A reader needs the application and the four datasets. The page reads the datasets with the rights of the person who opens it. Someone who can open the application but cannot see the datasets gets a page that stays empty.
  • The notebook runs with the rights of the person who runs it. That person must be able to read every source and to write the four datasets. The source data is owned by others: access to it is requested from its owners, person by person, and can take days.

Giving access

Access is given on the folder or on the object itself, with the sharing function of Skywise: open the folder that holds the object, select it, choose Share, add the person or the group, and choose the role (viewer, editor or owner). The simplest set-up is one folder for everything that belongs to the dashboard: sharing the folder shares what it holds.

When someone leaves the team, remove the person from the same places, or from the group that holds these rights. Nothing in the dashboard depends on a person's name.

A new reader

  1. Give viewer rights on the Slate application.
  2. Give viewer rights on the four datasets fto2_dash_… (or on the folder that holds them).
  3. Send the link of the dashboard, and the User guide.
  4. Check: ask the person to open the link. The page must show the date Données au… and figures. An empty page, or a box that names a query, means step 2 is missing.

A new person in charge

  1. Give editor rights on the Code Workspace and on the four datasets, and viewer rights on fto2_ai_labels and on the application.
  2. Have the person request access to the three sources: Non Conformity, Work_Order_Operation, MES Disruptions. Each dataset's page in Skywise shows who owns it and how to ask. Start early.
  3. Check, together: the person opens the Code Workspace with their own account, sees the notebook, the folder notebook_kit and the folder data with data/config, and runs the notebook from the first cell to the last. The last lines must list the four tables written.
  4. Check the end of the chain: a few minutes later, the date Données au… at the top of the dashboard is the day of the latest NC. If the notebook ran but the page did not move, the sync to Postgres did not follow: see the Technical manual.
  5. Give the Settings guide.
Files changed by one person, seen by the other The settings and the downloaded files live inside the Code Workspace. When two people use the workspace, check once that a file saved by one is seen by the other: one person changes the description of a line in a settings table and saves; the other reopens the workspace and looks. If the change is not there, the workspace keeps a copy per person until the changes are saved to the workspace itself (in the workspace: its command to save or synchronise the changes). Agree on who edits, and always save that way before leaving the workspace.

A new maintainer

  1. Everything of the person in charge, plus editor rights on the Slate application and on fto2_ai_labels.
  2. Check: the person opens the application in edit mode and sees the four queries and the code of each page.
  3. Give the Technical manual and the folder for_developers.

Before the owner leaves

A dashboard stops the day its only owner's account is closed. Go through this list with the successor at the keyboard, not from memory. Allow half a day, and do it at least a week before the departure: the requests for source data take time.

Where things are

  • Nothing is in a personal folder. The workspace, the four datasets, fto2_ai_labels and the application are in a folder of the team's project. What is in a personal folder is moved there (select, Move), and the dashboard is opened once after the move to check that it still shows data.
  • The inventory of chapter 1 is filled in.
  • The guides and the folder of the handover are stored where the team will find them, and the successor knows where.

Who owns it

  • Two owners at least, the leaving owner not counted, on the folder, the workspace, the datasets and the application.
  • The sync to Postgres of the four datasets does not depend on the leaving account. Look at who created it and whether it runs by itself after each update of the datasets; if it is tied to the leaving account, have the successor create it again under theirs.
  • No schedule runs under the leaving account. If the notebook or a dataset is refreshed by a schedule, its owner is changed.

The successor can do everything

  • The person in charge has run the notebook with their own account, from the first cell to the last, and the dashboard's date has moved.
  • The person in charge has changed a setting (for example the description of a line), run the notebook, and seen that it was read. The deputy sees the same file.
  • The maintainer has opened the application in edit mode.
  • A reader who never used the dashboard has opened it from the link.

What stays behind

  • The former application and the former outputs. While the former Slate application is still used, the notebook also writes its four former tables. Once it is retired, set garder_anciennes_sorties to non in data/config/2_parametres.csv and remove the former application.
  • What only the leaving owner knows is written down: where the downloaded files come from and who sends them, who to ask for the price of a part, who owns each source.

When access goes wrong

The symptom tells which link of the chain is missing.

What the person seesWhat is missingWhat to do
Skywise refuses the link of the dashboard.The application (link 5).Give viewer rights on the application.
The page opens but stays on the loading box, or a box names a query that returned nothing.The four datasets (link 3), or the sync (link 4).Open the same link yourself. If it works for you: give the person viewer rights on the four datasets. If it fails for you too: the sync or the notebook; see the Technical manual.
The page works but its date Données au… is old.Nothing: the update was not run.The person in charge runs the notebook.
The notebook stops at its first step with an error that names a dataset.A source (link 1).The person who runs it requests access to that dataset from its owner. Until then, another person in charge runs the notebook.
The notebook runs to the end, then fails when it writes.The four datasets (link 3).Give the person editor rights on the four datasets.
The notebook stops with a message that names a file and a line.Nothing: a typing mistake in a setting.Not an access problem. The message says what to correct (the Settings guide).
A setting changed by one person is not seen by another.Nothing: the change was not saved to the workspace.See the box in chapter 3.

The list on one page

To print and tick on the day of the handover.

  • Everything is in the team's project, nothing in a personal folder.
  • The inventory of chapter 1 is filled in: workspace, datasets, sync, application, guides.
  • Two owners besides the one who leaves.
  • The readers are a group, or a list that someone else can change.
  • The person in charge and a deputy have editor rights on the workspace and the four datasets.
  • The person in charge has access to Non Conformity, Work_Order_Operation, MES Disruptions and fto2_ai_labels.
  • The person in charge has run the notebook alone, and the dashboard's date has moved.
  • The person in charge has changed a setting and seen it read; the deputy sees the same file.
  • The sync to Postgres and any schedule do not depend on the leaving account.
  • The maintainer can open the application in edit mode and has the folder for_developers.
  • A new reader has opened the dashboard from the link.
  • The former application and the former outputs are retired, or a date is set to do it.
  • The names of the people who send the downloaded files and who own the sources are written down.
