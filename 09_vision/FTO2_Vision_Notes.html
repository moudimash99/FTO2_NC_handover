Findings

What the data says, in detail

Each finding gives the numbers, where it belongs in the dashboard and where the data comes from. Every figure below is computed by the prototype from the same rows, so you can change the filters there and see it move.

1
+16%

Per aircraft, 2026 is 16% above 2025, not 39%

2026 counts 722 NCs against 518 at the same date in 2025, +39%. Most of that is production: on the three lines with a 2025 base, 110 aircraft went through FTO2 against 93 (+18%), and the A320 line went from 2 aircraft to 23. Compared at equal production, with 2025 scaled line by line to the 2026 number of aircraft, NCs and defects per aircraft are both up 16%.

By quarter, NCs per aircraft were +5%, +36%, then +12% in Q3 (defects per aircraft +24%, +29%, then −3%). The lines split. The A350 is back to its 2025 level since July: 3.3 NCs per aircraft in Q3 2025, 3.4 in Q3 2026. The A330 stays 25% above, 6.1 to 7.6. The MFT actions started in July; that is a coincidence in time, not a proof, and for all lines together the move from +23% in the first half to +12% since July is within what chance gives over three months.

Corrected on 20 October. The first version compared raw counts (+39%, a gap "closing" from +50% to +11%), which mostly measured the extra aircraft, as you pointed out.

Where it goes: the overview. It replaces the placeholder 2025 bar, the fictitious target and the budget line with the real year at equal production, and gives every headline figure a comparison per aircraft.

Data: the 2025 Skywise export in 06_input_data/work_orders_disruptions_nc2025.rar on main, counted like 2026: FTO2 liable, not deleted, no ERMA, the same phase rule, cut at the same calendar date. Check: January 2026 is in both files, and 83 of the 86 January NCs of the 2025 file are in your extract. Aircraft: MSNs with at least one FTO2 NC in the period, until a 2025 production file replaces them.

2
805 / 813

The test bench is a near-perfect net for connection errors

805 of the 813 swapped wires, wrong terminals and unconnected wires were found at test, and none reached a customer this year in Série. What reaches the customer is damage (9 of 263 damaged-item defects, 3.4%), missing items (5 of 305, 1.6%) and wiring workmanship (16 of 1,007, 1.6%).

Where it goes: the customer returns tab. Today it is a separate page in the older band layout. Drawn as the end of one flow, from cause to the station that found it to the customer, it shows which barrier each cause slips through, and the customer returns stop being a list apart.

Data: ready. The station comes from cnq_where_found, already in fto2_nc.csv. Customer-return numbers are small (32 defects in Série), so the chart shows counts next to every rate.

3
4 h 22

An NC is mostly handling, not repair

In the cost model, an NC takes 4 h 22 of work: alert 30 min, writing 35, technical support 60, setup 44, repair 64, re-check 30. Over the year that is 3,149 hours, 765 of them hands-on. A one-defect NC costs 181 € per defect; a defect inside an inspection list costs 25 €. The Airbus standard method puts the same NCs at 363.6 k€, 2.2 times the per-defect detail you use.

Where it goes: the cost tab, in place of the bubble timeline and the unlabeled month chart. One bar that shows where the four hours go explains the cost model better than the folded explanation does.

Data: ready, from the cnq_min_* columns. These are model figures. The model on main (cnq-2026.10.19) moves time from test NCs to internal NCs; the chart follows whatever model the build uses.

4
20 / 68

The same places fail, aircraft after aircraft

113 locations (FIN) were hit on five aircraft or more this year, and they carry 1,128 of the 2,221 defects that name a location, 51%. Some are new: 300VT101 was hit on 4 of 50 A350 at this date in 2025, and on 20 of 68 this year. Some are chronic: 9KS and 115VM were hit on 15 and 16 of 50 A350 at this date in 2025, and on 18 of 68 this year.

The swapped wires say more. Where both pins are written, 44% of swaps are between two pins that follow each other, and 34 pin pairs came back on several aircraft (411VC001A pins 6 and 15 on four). The MFT action on swapped wires is tutoring. The pairs that repeat point to a second lever next to it: mark or key those pins on the connector.

Where it goes: the "Où" tab, next to the FIN recurrence matrix you already have: one line per location with its 2025 and 2026 share of aircraft, tagged new or chronic. Clicking a location shows its aircraft.

Data: ready. FIN from the parser, both years. The pins are read from the text (09_vision/pins.py): 390 of 479 swapped-wire defects name both pins. Without connector drawings, "follow each other" means consecutive numbers, not physical neighbours.

−9%

Per aircraft, cable defects went down while the rest went up

Scaled to the 2026 number of aircraft, 2025 would have had 265 cable defects at this date; 2026 has 241, −9%. Over the same aircraft, all defects went from 2,307 to 2,666 (+16%) and the other defects from 2,042 to 2,425 (+19%). Their share fell from 12.0% to 9.0%. Same parser and same scope on both years; the A320 is left out (2 aircraft in 2025).

Where it goes: the cable discussion first. It needs no change to the categories, so it holds whichever option (A to D) you choose. In the dashboard it is one small index chart beside the year comparison.

Data: ready. The cable flag of parser v4.8 on both years (243 in 2026 with the A320; the root-cause labels give 237).

Change

What holds the current dashboard back

Six things, from the screenshots of the 19 October version. None of them is a bug; each one costs the reader attention or trust.

1Placeholders that look like results

The card says "fictitious", but a reader sees five red "+33%" and remembers them. The same goes for the "2025 fictif (= 2026)" bar in the trend and the budget line drawn at forecast minus 25%. In a meeting, the first person to notice that all lines are exactly +33% stops trusting the rest of the page.

Change: use the real 2025 from the archive now, and show a target or a budget only once someone gives the figure. The prototype does the first part.

2Two colour systems on one dashboard

Change: one rule for colour on every tab. Green is FTO2, orange is cable and nothing else, red is what reaches the customer and alerts, grey is context and last year. Parts of a whole get shades of one hue. Products need no colour of their own, since their names are written beside the bars.

3Charts that do not earn their space

The "Mois de création" chart on the cost tab has no axis and no values. The cable card has two rows in a card sized for twenty. The bubble timeline shows that there are many NCs, which the figure above it already says.

Change: drop them, or give each one a question. The NC anatomy (finding 3) can take the bubble chart's place; the cable split can live inside the category card as the orange share it already is.

4Figures with nothing to compare to

"722 NC" alone says nothing about whether 722 is good. Each headline figure needs a reference next to it: the change against 2025 at the same date, and a small weekly trend that ends on the last four weeks.

5Pages that do not talk to each other

The customer returns tab is in the older band layout and does not connect to where the same causes are caught inside FTO2. The RCA tab is about 11,000 pixels of reference text: definitions belong in a panel that opens from any cause name, wherever it appears. The FIN matrix does not lead to the aircraft, and an aircraft does not lead back to its NCs.

6Titles that make the reader find the point

Titles name the chart ("Coût par famille de produit"), so the point of each chart is left to the presenter. A title computed from the data ("Le test attrape 99% des erreurs de branchement") does the first reading for the audience, and still respects the rule that nothing is typed by hand. The switch back to names keeps your current choice one click away.

Limits

What to keep in mind

  • 2025 has only the parser's labels. The year comparisons by cause use parser v4.8 on both years, so their 2026 values differ slightly from the root-cause labels used everywhere else (for example 243 cable defects against 237).
  • The A350's return to its 2025 level in Q3 coincides with the July actions. It does not prove they worked; the MFT follow-up page already tracks each action against its own cause.
  • Customer-return rates rest on 32 defects in Série (69 with REC and FTI). They show where to look, not a measured escape rate.
  • Every 2025 → 2026 comparison is at equal production: on each line, 2025 is scaled by aircraft 2026 ÷ aircraft 2025. Until a 2025 production file is available, "aircraft" means MSNs with at least one FTO2 NC in the period, and lines with fewer than 8 aircraft in 2025 (the A320) are left out of the comparisons.
  • Pins are read from the text for 390 of 479 swapped-wire defects. Consecutive numbers are not always physical neighbours.
  • Alerts use a Poisson test against the rest of the year, at 5% (amber) and 1% (red). With 25 causes, expect about one amber alert by chance on a quiet month.
  • The prototype is a separate page. Moving its parts into the dashboard goes through build/build_all.py and the template, tab by tab, as in the roadmap.
